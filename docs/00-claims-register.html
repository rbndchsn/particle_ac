Resolution Notes#

C2 — rejected, and why that matters#

The original claim said some physics is unobservable in principle because the trigger destroys data irreversibly under a theory-laden menu. That is wrong, and the research stream rejected it rather than defending it.

Reason A: ATLAS and CMS run zero-bias, enhanced-bias and pileup streams which are not selected on physics signatures. These give an effective prescale of roughly 40,000 — an unbiased sample of order 0.5–1 fb⁻¹, enough to set limits around the 3 fb level. Physics outside the menu is therefore badly sampled, not unobservable. The strong version of the claim is false and has been struck from the register.

Three further supporting arguments were also rejected: that the bandwidth constraint is a hard physical limit (LHCb and ALICE abandoned hardware triggers entirely, so it is an engineering choice); that the astrophysical detectors face the same problem (IceCube archives all raw waveforms); and that the field does not acknowledge the issue (it does, in print).

What survives is narrower, documented, and sharper — C2a and C2b above. The replacement claim is falsifiable by a single coverage integral over signature space with a stated measure. No such integral appears to exist.

This resolution is the frame working as designed. A register that only accumulated confirmations would be worthless.

Every claim in this archive carries an evidence tier. Claims examined and rejected are kept on the record rather than removed — including both hypotheses the project began with.
