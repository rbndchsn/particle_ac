Purpose#

A single register of what this archive actually asserts. Each claim has an ID, a tier, the file that supports it, and a status. Nothing may be carried into a published synthesis unless it appears here with a status of SUPPORTED or SUPPORTED-AS-INFERENCE.

Status values: UNTESTED · SUPPORTED · SUPPORTED-AS-INFERENCE · WEAKENED · REJECTED

Outcome#

Seven candidate claims were written before research began. Of those seven, three were rejected as stated (C1, C2, C4), one was split with half rejected (C6), and three survived in narrowed or reworded form (C3, C5, C7). Six narrower replacement claims were written from what the evidence actually supported.

Not one of the seven survived in its original wording. The two the project considered strongest going in — C1 and C2 — are both among the rejected.

Resolution Notes#

C1 — rejected, and the narrower claim that replaced it#

The original claim said combinations present ATLAS and CMS as independent while ignoring systematic error correlated through shared software. That is false, and documented to the contrary A. The top-quark mass combination assigns a correlation of 0.5 to hadronisation, colour reconnection, underlying event, QCD radiation and matrix-element generator terms, and 0.85 to PDF and pile-up, and publishes a per-category scan showing modelling shifts of a few MeV against a 0.33 GeV total. The Higgs combination correlates QCD-scale and parton-shower terms and deliberately decorrelates acceptance-theory terms estimated by different methods.

What survives is C1a, which is a question about grounds rather than about neglect: the coefficient looks calibrated on how differently the two collaborations work, not on how much model ancestry they share. For the top mass the distinction is numerically immaterial because the response is linear. Whether it matters anywhere else is untested, because no published study scans correlation and model variance together.

C2 — rejected, and why that matters#

The original claim said some physics is unobservable in principle. That is wrong A: zero-bias, enhanced-bias and pileup streams are not selected on physics signatures and give an effective prescale near 40,000 — an unbiased sample of order 0.5–1 fb⁻¹. Physics outside the menu is badly sampled, not unobservable.

Three supporting arguments also failed: that bandwidth is a hard physical limit (LHCb and ALICE abandoned hardware triggers, so it is an engineering choice); that astrophysical detectors share the problem (IceCube archives all raw waveforms); and that the field does not acknowledge the issue (it does, in print).

C2a is falsifiable by one coverage integral over signature space with a stated measure. No such integral appears to exist.

C3 — supported, narrowed#

The circularity is real and the field names it. ATLAS's own tuning documentation warns that over-tuning non-perturbative parameters may hide new physics A. In iterative unfolding the start values are the simulation's own truth, and the field states plainly that this dependence is the regularisation A. The two-generator spread used to estimate model uncertainty is documented as not bounding the spread to a third A.

Two stronger versions were rejected: that tunes are fitted to the signals they correct, and that unfolded spectra are prior-driven artefacts. Data-driven background estimation and control-region validation exist precisely to reduce simulation dependence, and they work.

C4 — rejected on mechanism, conclusion rebuilt#

Hyperauthorship is not what prevents external audit. Internal review as a rubber stamp was rejected, and the OPERA episode cuts the other way: a collaboration of that size published its own anomaly and then its own refutation.

The conclusion survives by a different route, and a better documented one A. The field's own reinterpretation inventory records full statistical likelihoods released for two analyses, efficiency maps in very few cases, data for refits often not provided, and machine-learning analyses that cannot be reproduced outside the collaboration that produced them. By 2025 the reproducibility of some earlier searches had been lost inside those collaborations. An outsider cannot audit because the artefacts are not there, not because three thousand people signed the paper.

C5 — supported, narrowed#

Two strong versions were rejected: that there is no conflict-of-interest regime (there is), and that no panel member lacks a stake (at least one documented counterexample).

What is documented A: the regime recuses four of thirty-two members, for national-laboratory employment, from a single acceptance vote; all thirty-two are particle or accelerator physicists; the European strategy mandate carries no conflict-of-interest provisions at all; and cost figures are taken from proponents, with the subcommittee barred from producing estimates of its own. The sharpest point is the last one, and it is not about anyone's motives.

C6 — split#

C6a holds A: one qualified Nb₃Sn supplier at HL-LHC specification against eight for ITER; roughly 93% of world niobium mine production from one country and 77–90% of that from one firm; two companies worldwide making helium turbo-expanders of the required size; zero US niobium production.

C6b does not. Member states receive 94% of direct contracts but 77% of value added, and Germany and the United Kingdom are documented as poorly balanced — that is, under-returned. Capture of procurement by the largest contributors was examined and rejected.

C7 — supported, reworded#

The original wording said the retirement was not reflected in successor-machine justifications. That overstates it. The retirement is explicit and on the record in the theory literature A: a sub-TeV bound published in 2008 with a stated failure condition, and the same author conceding a "post-naturalness era" in 2017.

What is uneven is the reflection. The 2023 US strategy report contains zero instances of "naturalness", "hierarchy problem", "fine-tuning" or "unnatural" across 379,741 characters while paraphrasing the argument those words name; the 2025 European briefing book restores the term. Also rejected: that naturalness was merely aesthetic (its effective-field-theory grounding is a real argument), and that the goalposts moved only after exclusions (split supersymmetry abandoned naturalness in 2004, before the LHC ran).

C1a — weakened by the evidential audit, and restated#

The first half of C1a is confirmed in the papers' own words: shared-modelling categories are assigned ρ = 0.5 and the justification given is that the experiments use different generators and different variation schemes — procedural difference, not shared lineage A.

The second half does not survive. The published sensitivity scans show the top-mass central value moving by at most 41 MeV under adverse correlation assumptions, against a 0.33 GeV total uncertainty, and the combination states explicitly that "No category was identified to have ρ=1" A. Whatever the grounds, the numerical consequence where it has been tested is small.

What remains is narrower and is now C1a′: no published scan of correlation assumptions exists for the 2012 discovery significance — the one quantity for which the independence of the two experiments was rhetorically load-bearing. Robustness demonstrated for a 2024 precision combination is being borrowed, implicitly, by a 2012 discovery claim. Falsified by production of such a study.
