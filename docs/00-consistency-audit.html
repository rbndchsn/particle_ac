Internal Consistency Audit

Where the archive contradicts itself, where its corrections sit behind the errors they correct, and where the public page claims more than the documents support

v12026-11-25active - first full internal audit; 28 findings, 6 of them capable of discrediting the archive in front of a specialist

Evidence tiers used in this document (16 labelled claims)

A Documented 15B Contested 0C Inference 1D Rejected 0

What the audit found

  • 28 inconsistencies across fourteen documents and the landing page, six of them the kind a specialist catches on first reading. All six were on the most-read page.
  • “Full statistical likelihoods for two analyses” was a 2020 snapshot printed as current; the archive's own 2025 source records 41. Off by a factor of twenty on the figure carrying the literature section.
  • The thesis asserted the naturalness argument “was removed” from funding documents. The archive says the vocabulary was dropped and the argument kept in paraphrase — and the page contradicted itself eight lines later.
  • Giudice's “a doctor unable to find the right diagnosis cannot simply declare the patient healed” was quoted as his concession. It is his counterargument — his refusal to drop the problem.
  • A citation bug corrupting published output: two reference continuation lines beginning “2004.” and “2013.” were parsed as list items, so the page rendered 46 entries against 44 and every in-text citation from 10 upward resolved to the wrong source.

Checked and sound

  • The load-bearing numbers check out across documents: 379,741 characters; 1.7% of €16.4bn; $162,018,139.47; 93% niobium; 1-vs-8 Nb₃Sn suppliers; the 40% premium; $13 against $7.50; 3 of 60; 282–143; 0.13 parts per trillion.
  • The cost-overrun baseline and the “not an outlier” conclusion are fully supported.
NoteThis audit is the archive auditing itself, and it found the same failure mode the archive documents in others: a correction filed at line 614 of a 682-line file, behind five live statements of the claim it retracts. Every finding here has been applied.

What this audit did, and what it is for#

This is not new research. No external source was consulted. The archive was read against itself: every load-bearing number was traced to every place it appears, every correction was located and its line number compared with the line numbers of the passages it corrects, and every factual assertion on the public landing page was traced back to the document that is supposed to carry it.

Line numbers are as of 2026-11-25 and will move as soon as any fix is applied. Apply fixes from the bottom of each file upward, or re-grep the quoted string.

Severity bands used below.

  • S1 - discrediting. A specialist reading the public page and then the document would find the page asserting something the document rejects, or asserting as current a figure the archive itself records as superseded. Six findings.
  • S2 - cross-document contradiction. Two documents in the archive say incompatible things, or assign incompatible evidence tiers to the same fact. Nine findings.
  • S3 - drift. Variant numbers, stale status lines, orphan references, overstated adjectives. Thirteen findings.

One structural note before the findings. The archive has issued exactly two corrections in prose: 06_dissent_register/legitimacy_confrontations_v1.md:614 and 03_hardware_systems/trigger_data_destruction_v1.md:54. One of the two is placed after everything it corrects. The other is placed well, and the public page then uses the uncorrected figure anyway. That pattern - correction written, correction not propagated - is the single most common defect found, and it accounts for five of the six S1 findings.

S1-1. The landing page states a 2020 snapshot as the current state of likelihood publication, and the archive's own 2025 source contradicts it#

File / line: site/content/index.md:70 and, in the same words, site/content/index.md:14.

Current text (line 70):

The field's own reinterpretation inventory records full statistical likelihoods released for two analyses. Efficiency maps in "very few cases." Data for refits "often not provided." Machine-learning analyses that "cannot be reproduced at all outside the experimental collaboration." By 2025, the reproducibility of some earlier searches had been lost inside those collaborations.

Current text (line 14, same claim, different wording):

A literature that has released full statistical likelihoods for two analyses, and that contains machine-learning results its own authors state cannot be reproduced outside the collaboration that made them.

What is wrong. The figure "two" comes from the 2020 LHC Reinterpretation Forum report, and 05_publication_governance/publication_governance_v1.md:162 carries the time qualifier that makes it true: "At the time of writing, ATLAS had published full likelihoods in JSON format for two analyses." The landing page drops the qualifier and sets the figure next to a 2025 statement, so it reads as current. It is not current, and the archive knows it is not current. 04_software_algorithmic/software_stack_bias_v1.md:607-611 says, under the heading "What has improved":

ATLAS released "the first full HistFactory models" in 2019, "met with enthusiasm" by the phenomenology community; a HEPData search found 41 statistical-model records (16 SUSY, 21 top), and "14 of the 16" SUSY searches with HistFactory models are reused by reinterpretation tools. CMS "has recently followed suit by publishing their Combine software" with the 2012 Higgs statistical model and "plans to systematically release full statistical models."

Forty-one against two. The page is off by a factor of twenty on the number that carries its "literature cannot check" section, and the correcting passage sits in a different file, 537 lines into it. 05_publication_governance/publication_governance_v1.md:807-808 even lists the question as an open gap - "Whether the Reinterpretation Forum's 2020 recommendations have since been adopted - in particular how many ATLAS and CMS analyses now publish full likelihoods, against the two" - which means the archive flagged the staleness and then published the stale number anyway.

Recommended replacement, site/content/index.md:70 (replace the first two sentences, keep the rest of the paragraph):

The field's own 2020 reinterpretation inventory recorded full statistical likelihoods released for two analyses; by the 2025 follow-up the count had reached 41 statistical-model records, with CMS releasing its Combine software and planning systematic release. The direction is right and the base was two. Efficiency maps in "very few cases." Data for refits "often not provided." Machine-learning analyses that "cannot be reproduced at all outside the experimental collaboration." By 2025, the reproducibility of some earlier searches had been lost inside those collaborations.

Recommended replacement, site/content/index.md:14:

A literature whose own inventory of reusable material counted full statistical likelihoods for two analyses in 2020 and forty-one in 2025 - and which still contains machine-learning results its own authors state cannot be reproduced outside the collaboration that made them.

Also fix, 00_method/claims_register_v1.md:100-103, which repeats the stale figure with no date. Current text:

The conclusion survives by a different route, and a better documented one A. The field's own reinterpretation inventory records full statistical likelihoods released for two analyses, efficiency maps in very few cases, data for refits often not provided, and machine-learning analyses that cannot be reproduced outside the collaboration that produced them.

Replacement:

The conclusion survives by a different route, and a better documented one A. The field's 2020 reinterpretation inventory recorded full statistical likelihoods released for two analyses, efficiency maps in very few cases, data for refits often not provided, and machine-learning analyses that cannot be reproduced outside the collaboration that produced them; the 2025 follow-up records 41 statistical-model records and a CMS commitment to systematic release, so the artefact deficit is narrowing and the claim must be dated.

S1-4. The landing page attributes the full 13,000x rejection to hardware acting in microseconds; the document says hardware does 400x and names the rest as software#

File / lines: site/content/index.md:26 and site/content/index.md:14.

Current text (line 26):

The LHC discards 13,000x of its collisions in hardware, in microseconds, unrecoverably - under a menu built, in the field's own words, from "theoretical models and detailed Monte Carlo simulations."

Current text (line 14):

An instrument that destroys thirteen thousand times more data than it keeps, in hardware, irreversibly, under a menu built from the theories it exists to test.

What is wrong. Three things, and the first is fatal.

  1. The 13,000x figure is the product of Level-1 and the High-Level Trigger, not the hardware stage. 03_hardware_systems/trigger_data_destruction_v1.md:48: "The resulting rejection factors for ATLAS Run 3, from the figures above: L1 about 400:1, HLT about 33:1, overall about 1.3 x 10^4:1." The hardware stage discards 400x, not 13,000x.
  2. The document explicitly forbids the conflation the page performs. 03_hardware_systems/trigger_data_destruction_v1.md:64-65 lists "Three distinct irreversibilities, which the argument should not conflate": "L1 rejection is hardware, fixed-latency, and final. This is the only stage that is literally destruction by hardware within microseconds. ... HLT rejection is software and equally permanent as to the record, but it is not hardware destruction. It occurs at second-to-minute timescales on a CPU farm."
  3. The figure is also not the archive's best current number for total loss. 03_hardware_systems/trigger_data_destruction_v1.md:54: "Counting all recorded streams, the Run 3 kept fraction is nearer 10^-3 than the 2.5 x 10^-5 implied by 1 kHz." That is nearer 1,000x. The page picks the largest available ratio and attaches it to the strongest available mechanism.

Separately, the 13,000x figure carries tier C in the source - "arithmetic on (3) and (4); the collaborations do not publish the product as a single figure" - and the page prints it in bold as a fact. That is also recorded below as S3-11.

Recommended replacement, site/content/index.md:26:

The LHC keeps roughly one collision in 13,000 - about 400:1 of that thrown away by fixed-latency hardware in microseconds, the rest by software on a farm, both unrecoverably - under a menu built, in the field's own words, from "theoretical models and detailed Monte Carlo simulations."

Recommended replacement, site/content/index.md:14:

An instrument that destroys thousands of times more data than it keeps, the first cut made in hardware within microseconds and never revisited, under a menu built from the theories it exists to test.
