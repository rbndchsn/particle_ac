Method

Research Frame and Evidence Standards

How this investigation is structured, what counts as evidence, and the methodological traps built into its own premise

v12026-11-24active — governs all other files in this archive

The Research Question#

Across the full stack of high-energy particle physics — theoretical motivation, machine hardware, detector instrumentation, simulation and analysis software, publication, and funding governance — where are the weaknesses, and are they acknowledged?

The investigation operates at five levels, each with its own folder:

  1. Theory — whether the notions motivating the machines are falsifiable, and what happens to a motivating argument when its prediction fails.
  2. Hardware — what the instruments physically cannot see, and who builds them.
  3. Software — what theoretical assumptions are encoded in the code that converts detector signals into physics claims.
  4. Publication — whether the literature is structurally capable of auditing itself.
  5. Governance — who decides what gets built, and what their interest is.

The Trap in This Project's Own Premise#

The brief is to "expose weaknesses." That phrasing pre-commits to a conclusion, which is the same methodological failure the investigation intends to document in others. A critique built on motivated reasoning can be dismissed on exactly the grounds it advances.

Two consequences follow, and they are binding on every file here:

Tier D is mandatory. Every research file must close with claims that were examined and could not be supported. A file with no Tier D section is incomplete, not strong. The presence of rejected claims is what distinguishes forensic work from advocacy, and it is the only thing that makes the surviving claims cost anything to dismiss.

Mundane explanations get priority over motivated ones. Budget constraint, institutional inertia, path dependence, and technical conservatism explain most of what looks like suppression. An explanation involving interest or capture must beat the mundane explanation on evidence, not on narrative appeal.

A Terminological Correction#

"Bias in the algorithms" imports a meaning from machine-learning ethics — social bias in training data — that does not describe what happens here and would mislead a technical reader.

The actual phenomenon is theoretical prior encoded in instrumentation and code: the assumptions of a model are built into the apparatus and software that are then used to test that model. This is a sharper, more defensible, and more serious claim than "bias." The archive uses the precise formulation throughout. Where a genuine selection or representational bias exists, it is named as such and distinguished.

Evidence Tiers#

Every substantive claim in every file carries a tier label.

Tier Meaning Standard of proof
A Documented and acknowledged inside the field Primary literature, collaboration technical design reports, official lab or agency documents, procurement records
B Contested inside the field, named advocates on both sides Both positions cited from their own authors, not from characterisations
C Structural inference — follows from Tier A facts but has not been assembled or tested by the field Must state the Tier A facts it rests on and what would falsify it. Never presented as a finding.
D Examined and rejected Recorded with the reason it failed, so it is not re-litigated

Tier C is where this project's original contribution lives, and it is also the most dangerous tier. A Tier C claim that is presented as established converts the whole archive into polemic. Tier C claims must be written so that a hostile specialist reading them would say "that inference is available from those facts," not "that is unsupported."

Falsifiability Requirement#

Each level of analysis must state what would show its critique to be wrong:

  • Theory critique fails if the motivating arguments were in fact abandoned openly and on the record when their predictions failed, with the abandonment reflected in subsequent funding requests.
  • Hardware critique fails if the instrumental blind spots are published, quantified, and factored into discovery claims.
  • Software critique fails if generator-choice and simulation-dependence uncertainties are propagated with correlations between experiments correctly treated.
  • Publication critique fails if there is a working mechanism by which an outside specialist can audit a multi-thousand-author result and force a correction.
  • Governance critique fails if strategy bodies include members with no prospective stake, and if their recommendations have historically gone against the interest of their own membership.

These are genuine tests. Some may be passed. Recording that is the job.

Scope Boundaries#

In scope: terrestrial accelerators and their detectors; the software stack converting signals to claims; the publication and funding systems around them; astrophysical particle-acceleration instruments where the same issues recur.

Out of scope, and explicitly so: any claim that experimental results are fabricated, that the Standard Model's confirmed predictions are invalid, or that detector physics is faked. These are not weak versions of the argument; they are a different and unsupportable argument, and including them would discredit the defensible material by association. The Standard Model is the most precisely tested theory in the history of physics. The critique here concerns what surrounds that core: motivation, instrumentation limits, encoded assumptions, accountability, and interest.

One standing exception to the Tier D rule#

10_financial_investigation/ is a collection-phase document and deliberately carries no "Claims Examined and Not Supported" section. It was commissioned on the explicit instruction that collection and assessment be separated, and that nothing encountered be dropped — including claims from weak or anonymous sources, which are recorded with their provenance rather than filtered out.

In place of Tier D it closes with an enumerated list of what could not be established, each item naming the document that would answer it. Every entry carries a source class (audit, court, official, press, advocacy, anonymous) and a status (verified, reported, alleged, unverifiable), so the assessment pass has what it needs.

This is an exception to the frame, not a lapse from it. A later pass should not "fix" it by adding a rejection section — the assessment belongs in a separate document, against the collected material.

Every claim in this archive carries an evidence tier. Claims examined and rejected are kept on the record rather than removed — including both hypotheses the project began with.
