Weaknesses in the Theoretical Notions Motivating Particle Accelerators

Naturalness, supersymmetry, WIMPs and the landscape — what was predicted, what was excluded, and whether the motivating arguments were retired on the record

What this stream found

  • Naturalness carried a pre-registered falsification condition set inside the field: an explicit sub-TeV bound published with the statement that finding no such phenomena would mean the criterion failed. The condition was met.
  • The same author conceded the point in 2017 — “a doctor who is unable to find the right diagnosis cannot simply declare the patient healed.”
  • The 2023 US strategy report contains zero instances of “naturalness”, “hierarchy problem”, “fine-tuning” or “unnatural” across all 379,741 characters, while still paraphrasing the argument.
  • The fine-tuning threshold moved by stipulation across three decades (Δ=10 → Δ=20 → 100, with 1000 “or higher” acceptable) while gluino limits rose from ~310 GeV to ~2.3 TeV.
  • 750 GeV diphoton: global significances of 2.1σ and 1.6σ were stated in the abstracts; 200–350 theory papers followed anyway.

Examined and struck out

  • That naturalness was merely aesthetic — its effective-field-theory grounding is a real argument and survives.
  • That no-lose rhetoric is empty — the Higgs no-lose claim succeeded in 2012.
  • That axion research was funding-suppressed — DOE selected 4:1 WIMP:axion from 10:3 submissions, which is proportionate.
  • That goalposts moved only after exclusions — split SUSY abandoned naturalness in 2004, before the LHC.

What This Stream Was Asked to Test#

The research frame assigns this folder one candidate claim, C7: that motivating arguments whose predictions failed — naturalness and WIMP parameter space — were retired without the retirement being reflected in the justification for successor machines. The frame also states the condition under which this stream's critique fails: it fails if the motivating arguments were in fact abandoned openly and on the record when their predictions failed, with the abandonment reflected in subsequent funding requests.

That test turns out to be answerable with primary documents, and the answer is split. The abandonment is on the record in the theory literature and is unusually explicit. It is partly, but not fully, reflected in machine-justification documents, and the pattern of how it is reflected is itself the most interesting finding here.

Naturalness Before the LHC: A Criterion With a Stated Falsification Condition#

The pre-LHC statement of naturalness that matters most is Giudice's 2008 review, because it is from inside the field, is pre-data, and states both a numerical scale and a falsification condition.

Giudice distinguishes two things that both get called naturalness A. Aesthetic "structural naturalness" he describes as "a powerful inspirational principle but, of course, it cannot be used to validate a theory" (1). The criterion he then develops is quantitative "numerical naturalness," formulated as the rule that a dimensionless parameter "is allowed to be much smaller than unity only if setting it to zero increases the symmetry of the theory" (1).

From that he derives a scale. Requiring that the quantum correction to the Higgs mass not exceed 182 GeV, he writes, "implies Lambda < 1.0 TeV," and concludes that "Beyond the TeV a new theory should set in" (1) A.

He then states the test. "Only the LHC will tell us if the naturalness criterion is successful in this case as well," and — explicitly — if "experiments at the LHC find no new phenomena linked to the TeV scale, the naturalness criterion would fail" (1) A.

This is the single most important document for this stream. It establishes that naturalness was advanced, by one of its most careful expositors, as an empirically falsifiable criterion with a pre-registered failure condition and a numerical scale. It was not offered as mere taste.

The quantitative threshold has a documented origin. Barbieri and Giudice (1988) proposed upper bounds on superparticle masses by fixing the tolerable fine-tuning at a specific value, Delta = 10, from which "weakly interacting superparticles should be found in the regime 100-200 GeV" while strongly interacting ones "may become heavier than 1 TeV" (6, 7) A. Fischer's history notes plainly that for the choice Delta = 10 "There is no specific justification for this value" (7) A.

The narrower community statement of the naive expectation is tighter still. Craig's 2022 Snowmass white paper records that naive naturalness implies new physics "should enter around Lambda <= 500 GeV" (5) A.

Was Naturalness an Empirical or an Aesthetic Criterion?#

This is genuinely contested inside the field, and the honest answer is that the question is ill-posed as a binary, because at least two distinct criteria share the name.

Porter Williams separates an "autonomy of scales" requirement — which prohibits "sensitive dependence of an effective field theory's low-energy observables" on the cutoff-scale description — from a statistical principle that requires parameter values be "likely" under a chosen measure (3) B. He argues the historical motivations were "not, for example, solely or even primarily based on aesthetics" (3), that before the LHC results naturalness had "a reasonably good, but defeasible, motivation" (3), and that the statistical, multiverse-based version is "naturalness" in name only (3).

Hossenfelder's critique attacks the statistical version directly. Her argument is that "An unnatural number requires explanation when it is in a quantifiable sense unlikely," and that since no probability distribution over laws of nature is available — "We have only one set of laws of nature" — "the corresponding problems of naturalness are ill-defined and might not be problems at all" (4) B. She closes off the Bayesian escape: "Bayesian inference just moves the problem from the choice of a probability distribution to the choice of priors" (4). Her verdict: "Naturalness is hence either ill-defined or meaningless," and "Arguments from naturalness are then merely aesthetic criteria with little historical evidence of being useful" (4).

Note that Williams and Hossenfelder are not simply opposed. Williams concedes that "the most straightforward interpretation of present data is that the naturalness ideology is wrong" is a reasonable reading if naturalness means autonomy of scales (3) B. Hossenfelder states that "the data delivered by the LHC has now ruled out a technically natural explanation for the Higgs mass" (4) B. The disagreement is about whether what remains deserves the name, not about whether the TeV-scale expectation failed.

A hostile reading of the critique should be anticipated here: pointing at the aesthetic version and then treating the whole criterion as aesthetic is the motte-and-bailey move in reverse, and this file does not make it. Giudice's and Williams's effective-field-theory grounding is a real argument, and it survives.

Was the Retirement Explicit and On the Record?#

Yes in the theory literature, and the explicitness is striking.

Giudice's own 2017 paper is titled for it. He names "the present period of rethinking the directions in particle physics as the post-naturalness era" (2) A and states that "the lack of new physics in the initial stages of the LHC project is putting into question the logic of naturalness when applied to the Higgs" (2).

Craig's Snowmass white paper is blunter on the specific prediction: "At this point it is fair to say that supersymmetry did not appear where naturalness arguments led us to expect it" (5) A.

But both documents retain the problem while conceding the prediction. This is the field's strongest counterargument and it must be stated at full strength. Giudice: "If the LHC rules out dynamical solutions to Higgs naturalness at the weak scale, it does not eradicate the problem," and "a doctor who is unable to find the right diagnosis cannot simply declare the patient healed" (2). His conclusion is that "the concept of naturalness cannot simply be ignored, although its use may differ from what it has been so far" (2) B. Craig parallels it: the electroweak scale's survival "does not mean that the electroweak scale is unnatural, or that naturalness itself has failed," because "the failure of a specific answer to a naturalness problem does not signify the failure of the problem itself" (5) B. Craig still describes naturalness as "a pragmatic and empirically-validated strategy for discovering new physics" and says "the motivation for thinking about naturalness remains strong" (5).

This distinction is defensible. The observation that the Higgs mass is radiatively unstable in the Standard Model as an effective theory is a fact about the theory, not a prediction that failed. What failed is a specific inference from that fact to a mass scale.

One further fact cuts against any claim that the retreat was constructed after the exclusions arrived. Arkani-Hamed and Dimopoulos published split supersymmetry in May 2004 — seven years before LHC collisions produced meaningful exclusions — and it abandons the naturalness motivation outright A. Their abstract treats the cosmological constant as "a failure of naturalness," concludes that given a landscape of vacua "low energy SUSY becomes unnecessary," and states that confirmation would "demonstrate that supersymmetry is present but irrelevant for the hierarchy problem" (9). The intellectual exit from naturalness was built and published before the data demanded it.

What Happened in the Machine-Justification Documents#

This is where C7 is actually tested, and the result is a documented shift in vocabulary whose interpretation is contested.

2019, FCC Conceptual Design Report Volume 1. The hierarchy problem is used directly as motivation: "This puzzle is known as the 'Hierarchy Problem' and it galvanises the need to understand the origin of the Higgs potential" (35) A. The same volume states "Several theories that address the hierarchy problem will also be conclusively tested," while also recording, honestly, that "For supersymmetry, the mass reach of FCC-hh pushes beyond the regions motivated by the hierarchy problem alone" (35).

2019/2020, CERN Physics Briefing Book (input to the 2020 European Strategy). "the hierarchy problem between the mass of the Higgs boson and the Planck scale still remains a strong argument" (36) A. And: "Higgs naturalness remains a crucial open question" (36). The document frames the null results as strengthening rather than weakening the case: "the lack of evidence for new physics has widened the gap between Lambda and mh, making the tension more severe," and "The increasing tension could well herald an exciting change of paradigm" (36) B.

That last move deserves naming. A failed prediction is being read as increased evidence for the research programme that generated it. There is a legitimate version of this argument — if the problem is real, a wider gap is a sharper problem — but it is structurally the same shape as an unfalsifiable response, and the document does not distinguish the two.

2023, US P5 report "Exploring the Quantum Universe." I searched the full document, all 379,741 characters, in four overlapping windows. The strings "naturalness," "hierarchy problem," "fine-tuning," "fine tuned," "unnatural" and "guaranteed" do not appear anywhere in it A. The argument is nonetheless present in paraphrase: "We do not know why the Higgs boson mass should be as low as it is," and a "fantastical accident is required to accommodate the observed mass of the Higgs boson" (30) A.

2025, FCC integrated programme physics manifesto. No use of "naturalness," "hierarchy problem" or "fine-tuning." The nearest formulations are "theoretical puzzles, such as the low value of the Higgs mass" and the possibility of "an unnatural value of the weak scale or an ingenious, but still elusive structure" (38) A. The justification offered is exploration and precision, not a predicted scale: the programme is "the best possible opportunity for discovering physics beyond the Standard Model," with the goal to "Improve by an order of magnitude the direct discovery reach" (38).

November 2025, Physics Briefing Book for the 2026 European Strategy update. The term is back. "This is the essence of the 'naturalness' or 'hierarchy' problem," and the document states that "the naturalness problem, centred on the Higgs boson mass, is a theoretical puzzle but a concrete experimental programme as well," which "future colliders are uniquely positioned to pursue" (37) A. The sections I read did not discuss the LHC null results against naturalness-motivated models at the point where naturalness is used as motivation. I read roughly 40 percent of this 500,000-character document and did not reach its beyond-the-Standard-Model chapter; see Tier D.

Assessment of C7. Partly supported, with an important correction. The retirement was explicit on the record in the theory literature (1, 2, 5) and even pre-dated the data (9). It was not carried into the 2019 machine-justification documents, which continued to call the hierarchy problem "a strong argument" (36). By 2023 the US funding document had dropped the vocabulary entirely while keeping the argument in paraphrase (30), and the 2025 FCC manifesto did likewise (38) — but the 2025 European Briefing Book restored it (37). So the claim "retired without the retirement being reflected" is too strong: the reflection happened, unevenly, in some documents and not others, and it took the form of dropping a word rather than withdrawing an argument.

Supersymmetry: The Threshold Moved, With Numbers and Dates#

The sequence is documentable.

Date Source Naturalness threshold or exclusion
1988 Barbieri and Giudice (6, 7) Fine-tuning threshold set at Delta = 10; weak-sector superparticles predicted at 100-200 GeV
late 1990s Chan et al., per (7) Threshold moved up to Delta = 20
2008 Giudice (1) Lambda < 1.0 TeV from requiring the Higgs correction stay under 182 GeV
2008 Tevatron, DO with 2.1 fb-1 mSUGRA (10) Squark > 379 GeV, gluino > 308 GeV; 390 GeV for degenerate masses
2013 Feng (8) Naturalness bound m(gluino) <= 1.4 TeV x (Nmax/100)^(1/2); stops <= 1.0-1.1 TeV at Nmax = 100; informal survey finds Nmax of 100, 1000 "or higher" acceptable
2019 (PDG cutoff) PDG Supersymmetry Part II (11) Gluino excluded below about 2.3 TeV for a massless neutralino; first/second-generation squarks about 1.9 TeV; stop about 1.2 TeV
2022 Craig (5) "supersymmetry did not appear where naturalness arguments led us to expect it"

Three things in that table are worth separating.

First, the exclusions are real and large A. The gluino limit moved from roughly 310-390 GeV in 2008 (10) to about 2.3 TeV by 2019 (11) — a factor of six to seven.

Second, the tolerated fine-tuning threshold moved by a larger factor than is usually stated, and it moved by stipulation rather than by derivation. From Delta = 10 in 1988 (6, 7) to Delta = 20 by the late 1990s (7) to a working Nmax = 100 with 1000 "or higher" reported as acceptable in 2013 (8) A. Fischer's history describes the pattern as "the surpassing of expected upper-bounds has led to a series of adjustments to the naturalness principle" (7) A.

Third — and this is arithmetic I performed, not a published figure — inverting Feng's own relation at the 2019 gluino limit gives the implied tuning. Taking m(gluino) = 2.3 TeV in m(gluino) <= 1.4 TeV x (Nmax/100)^(1/2) yields Nmax of approximately 270. So the gluino exclusion alone implies tolerating roughly one part in 270, against the one part in ten that originally defined the bound: a factor of about 27. C, resting on (8) and (11).

Is this illegitimate goalpost drift, or legitimate parameter-space narrowing? The field's answer is that it is neither, because the thresholds were never derivable. Feng is explicit: "it is perfectly possible for values of parameters realized in nature to be unnatural," and "Naturalness is not a measure of our experimental knowledge of the parameters of nature" (8) B. He states that all naturalness bounds depend on subjective choices of framework, of which parameters count as fundamental, and of the sensitivity definition (8), and he cautions against "grand conclusions based on hard cutoffs in naturalness measures" (8, 7).

Taken at face value, that defence concedes the substance of the criticism. If the threshold was never derivable, then the 1988 prediction of 100-200 GeV was never a prediction in the sense that could have been wrong — yet it was cited as a prediction for two decades, and in 2008 was still being converted into the specific statement that new physics must set in below 1 TeV or naturalness fails (1). Fischer locates the flaw in the original framing rather than in the revisions: the upper-bounds argument was presented as a win-win, and the premise that SUSY's violating naturalness "would be reason enough to reject" SUSY is "clearly too strong" (7) B.

The 750 GeV Diphoton Excess: A Quantified Case Study#

Timeline and numbers A:

  • 15 December 2015: ATLAS and CMS present 13 TeV results including a diphoton excess near 750 GeV (16).
  • ATLAS, 3.2 fb-1 of 2015 data: local significance 3.9 sigma for the spin-0 search and 3.8 sigma for spin-2; global significance estimated at 2.1 sigma for both (12).
  • CMS, 8 and 13 TeV data: "The local significance of the excess is approximately 3.4 standard deviations," reduced to 1.6 sigma globally (13).
  • March 2016, a theory paper mid-episode: "More than 200 papers followed to date," with the first theory papers appearing "already on the day after the announcement of the excess" (14).
  • Matthew Buckley, writing as a working particle theorist: "more than 350 papers have appeared on the arXiv," and "The community's response to these findings has been truly electric" (15).
  • August 2016: the excess is absent in the new data (16, 17). Buckley: "The anomaly disappeared without a trace. With that, it was over" (15).
  • A secondary account puts the total at "about 500 theoretical studies" (17); CERN Courier says only "an influx of hundreds of papers" (16).

The exact count is not settled and I could not obtain an authoritative one; see Tier D. Floor and ceiling from primary and semi-primary sources are 200 (mid-episode, March 2016) and 350 (Buckley, retrospective).

What makes this a case study rather than an anecdote is that the global significances were published alongside the local ones from the start A. CERN Courier states the point plainly: "the significance of the excess was only 2 sigma and 1.6 sigma respectively" (16). Both collaborations reported, at the time, that after accounting for the search's scope the excess was at or below the 2 sigma level (12, 13). Hundreds of theory papers were written on a 2 sigma result whose 2 sigma character was in the abstracts.

Buckley is candid about the incentive asymmetry A. Theorists, he writes, face few constraints: "If we want to go down a rabbit hole that may not pan out, we have no one to answer to but ourselves" — while "keeping in mind the opinion of our peers, who are the people responsible for hiring or tenure decisions" (15). Experimentalists face a different constraint: "no one is allowed to just take the ATLAS detector for a spin to test their pet theory" (15).

The 5-Sigma Convention and the Look-Elsewhere Effect#

The convention is a convention, and the field's own statistics authorities say so.

Louis Lyons sets out four reasons for requiring 5 sigma A: the history of "many 'phenomena' that corresponded to 3 or 4 sigma effects that have gone away when more data were collected"; the look-elsewhere effect, where "the restriction on the location of the background fluctuation is removed" and the global p-value grows; a "subconscious Bayes' factor," since "Even if the likelihood ratio favours H1, we would still prefer H0 if our prior belief in H1 was very low"; and systematics, because "it is in general more difficult to estimate systematic uncertainties than statistical ones" (18).

Lyons then argues against his own field's uniform application of it A: "there are several reasons why it is not sensible to use a uniform criterion of 5 sigma for all searches for new physics," calling the fixed threshold "far too blunt a tool" and noting "there is often a degree of subjectivity in specifying the exact magnitude of the effect" (18). He offers graded significance levels "rather than being a rigid set of rules" (18).

The look-elsewhere effect has a standard treatment, Gross and Vitells (19), in which the trial factor is the ratio of global to local p-values. Eilam Gross's own teaching slides work the 750 GeV case explicitly A: from a local 3.9 sigma, the one-dimensional scan gives a global significance of about 2.7 sigma, and the two-dimensional scan over mass and width gives 2.1 sigma, which he describes as "not something to write home about" (20). The implied trial factor in that example is roughly 40 to 100 (20).

Two observations follow, and they point in opposite directions. The machinery for correcting the illusion existed, was published, was applied by the collaborations and was taught by the person who formalised it — the field handled this correctly A. And the corrected numbers did not govern community behaviour.

WIMP Dark Matter: The Exclusion Progression and the Neutrino Fog#

Spin-independent WIMP-nucleon cross-section limits at 90 percent confidence, from the collaboration papers A:

Date Experiment Limit At mass Exposure
Jul 2012 XENON100 (23) 2 x 10^-45 cm2 55 GeV 224.6 live days x 34 kg
Aug 2016 LUX complete exposure (24) 1.1 x 10^-46 cm2 50 GeV 3.35 x 10^4 kg-day, combined
May 2018 XENON1T (25) 4.1 x 10^-47 cm2 30 GeV 1.0 tonne-year
Oct 2024 LZ (26) 2.2 x 10^-48 cm2 40 GeV 4.2 tonne-years, 280 live days

That is a factor of about 900 in twelve years with no detection. The Snowmass Cosmic Frontier report puts the longer arc at xenon TPCs having "led the direction detection field across more than 3 orders of magnitude" (28) A.

The endpoint is now defined by neutrinos. O'Hare's redefinition sets the floor as the contour where the limit stops following Poisson scaling: the fog is where the limit scales as sigma proportional to N^(-1/n) with n greater than 2, and the floor is the n = 2 contour (27) A. Critically, the field does not treat this as a hard wall: the fog "imposes a gradual penalty that can be overcome, at least to some extent" (28), though the irreducible backgrounds mean "substantially diminished returns on further increases in exposure" (28).

Now the part that bears on whether the paradigm's persistence is evidence-driven. The field's own goal statements have moved and have been restated in coverage language rather than discovery language A:

  • Snowmass 2021 sets a 1 ktonne-year xenon target that "would fully explore the accessible WIMP parameter space as constrained by the neutrino fog" for masses above about 5 GeV (28). It also says DarkSide-20k would "lead to either discovery, confirmation, or exclusion of the WIMP dark matter hypothesis" in its range (28).
  • The same report states, for the case where nothing is seen: "Substantial well-motivated parameter space will yet remain" (28) A.
  • P5 2023 frames the fog as a milestone, not a verdict: "Reaching this so-called neutrino fog is a milestone, at which we cover a large fraction of WIMP theories" (30) A — a large fraction, not all.
  • P5 then routes the remainder to a new machine: "Testing the full range of WIMP theories could require a collider with at least 10 TeV pCM," and "Reaching the 10 TeV scale is needed to achieve definitive covering of the thermal targets for minimal WIMP candidates" (30) A.

The structure is legible and the documents are not hiding it: as each generation of detectors arrives empty, the completion criterion is restated at the next technology's reach, and when direct detection runs out of room the completion criterion moves to a collider. Whether that is drift or honest accounting of what the parameter space actually contains is contested B. A defender's case is strong here: the WIMP parameter space is a two-dimensional region, limits are curves across it, and a curve moving down genuinely does leave untested region below. Nothing about the physics requires the region to be exhausted by any one technology.

On persistence, there is a bibliometric measurement. Allzén's 2026 study of 201,627 ADS records finds that the WIMP-related umbrella remains the top-ranked dark matter candidate by a spectral centrality measure in both 2006-2015 and 2016-2025, while its cross-field structural role weakened sharply — normalized cross-field betweenness falling from 0.073 to 0.031 (42) A. The author is explicit that these measure attention and discursive connection, "not evidence of endorsement or evidential integration" (42), and that qualification should be preserved when the figures are cited.

Funding for Alternatives#

I could not assemble a defensible dollar comparison between WIMP searches and alternatives such as modified gravity; see Tier D. What I did obtain is the selection record for the DOE Second-Generation Dark Matter programme A.

Of 13 proposals received, 10 were WIMP and 3 were axion. Five were selected: ADMX-Gen 2 (axion), LZ, SuperCDMS-SNOLAB, DarkSide-G2 and COUPP-500 (all WIMP). The approved cost profile was 7 million dollars R&D plus 31 million fabrication, totalling 38 million across FY13-FY16 (29).

This does not show selection bias against alternatives. The selected 4:1 WIMP-to-axion ratio tracks the 10:3 submission ratio. The mundane explanation — that the community submitted mostly WIMP proposals, and that axion detector technology was at a different readiness level — accounts for the outcome without any appeal to capture. Per the research frame's priority rule, that explanation stands unless beaten on evidence, and I did not beat it.

String Landscape and the Swampland: Predictive Content#

The vacuum count is itself the finding. Michael Douglas, writing a review of the landscape, reports that "estimates range from 10^500, which at the time it was made seemed large, to the recent 10^272,000" (31) A.

What the landscape predicts, by its own reviewer's account, is hedged in both directions A. Douglas writes that "a combination of phenomenological and landscape arguments favor low energy supersymmetry around the scale 30-100 TeV," while adding that vacuum counting "will prefer low energy supersymmetry, though this is by no means proven" (31) — and, in a different scenario he considers, that "the discovery of low energy supersymmetry would be evidence against string theory" (31). He acknowledges unresolved structural gaps: "Even if the vacuum counting measure leads to definite predictions, we still need to estimate the cosmological factor," and "it will be some time before we can have any confidence in statements" requiring knowledge of all vacua (31).

A framework in which both the discovery and the non-discovery of low-energy supersymmetry can be read as support or as counter-evidence, depending on which sub-scenario is selected after the fact, has limited predictive content on that question. That is a reading of (31), not an accusation: Douglas states the scenarios and their conditions openly.

The swampland programme is the better-behaved case and the critique should concede it. Danielsson and Van Riet are explicit that their position is not proven — "it is far from established that there exists a landscape of de Sitter vacua in string theory," "a full proof that classical dS are always be tachyonic is not (yet) found," "there is no proof that such examples can not exist" — and they state a clean falsification route: one explicit de Sitter construction "that is explicit enough for it to be scrutinized to death" would settle it (32) A. They also claim a concrete observational consequence: the programme "really already made its first prediction: no measurable tensor modes in the CMB" (32) B. Whether that prediction is specific enough to be a test is arguable; that it is offered as one, with a stated refutation condition, is not.

"No-Lose Theorem" Rhetoric in Proposal Documents#

Actual instances, from primary documents A:

Espinosa and Gunion, 1998. For an e+e- linear collider at 500 GeV with TESLA-class luminosity, and "Assuming perturbativity up to a high energy scale ~ 10^16 GeV," the machine "will detect a Higgs boson signal regardless of the complexity of the Higgs sector and of how the Higgs bosons decay" (33). The theorem is conditional on perturbativity to the GUT scale and on the aggregate Higgs coupling-squared to ZZ being at least the Standard Model value (33).

Snowmass 2001, Working Group P1 Executive Summary. "at least one of the Higgs bosons of the MSSM is guaranteed to be discovered at either the Tevatron and/or the LHC"; "there is no escape route for the SM Higgs boson at the LHC"; and for a linear collider in extreme scenarios, "the 'no-lose' theorem still applies — Higgs production at the LC must be observable" (34).

FCC Conceptual Design Report Volume 1, 2019. "perhaps the biggest guaranteed physics goal of the FCC programme"; "guaranteeing a discovery or ruling them out"; "The FCC covers the full mass range for the discovery of these WIMP Dark Matter candidates"; "The top scalar partners will be discovered up to masses of close to 10 TeV" (35).

Two corrections to any critique built on this material, both mandatory.

First, the narrow no-lose claim was vindicated. The Standard Model and MSSM Higgs no-lose statements of 1998 and 2001 (33, 34) asserted that a Higgs boson would be found, and one was, in 2012. The rhetoric was not empty.

Second, the same Snowmass document that states the guarantees also states their limits: "there is no complete guarantee that at least one Higgs boson of the NMSSM must be discovered at the LHC for all choices of the model parameters," and for strongly coupled electroweak symmetry breaking it claims only that the physics "can only be fully explored at multi-TeV lepton and proton facilities" (34) A. The caveats are in the same executive summary as the guarantees.

What remains after those corrections is narrower but real: guarantee language migrated from theorem-grade claims with stated premises (33, 34) to coverage claims about dark matter parameter space and superpartner mass reach that are guarantees only within a chosen model class (35). The 2025 documents drop it: the FCC manifesto contains no guarantee language at all, framing its targets as opportunities (38) A.

Confirmation-Bias Clustering in Historical Measurement Plots#

This is the topic where the literature is weakest in the critique's favour, and it should be reported that way.

The qualitative pattern is documented. Holmes's history records that results "tend to cluster around a certain value for many years" and then "suddenly jump to cluster around a new value," with "Two such jumps occur for the speed of light" (22) A. Klein and Roodman identify "the 17 km/sec shift between the series of experiments from 1930-1940 and later determinations" (21) A. For CP violation, Holmes notes that measurements of the relevant amplitude "before 1973 are systematically different from those after 1973" (22) A.

Mechanism is named, by experimenters, in their own words. Dunnington in 1932 warned that "It is easier than is generally realized to unconsciously work toward a certain value," and built his apparatus so he could not know his result until analysis was complete (21) A. The post-mortem mechanism Klein and Roodman cite is stopping bias: the investigator searches for error sources and "continues" searching until the result matches the accepted value (21), against Galison's observation that "there is no strictly logical termination point inherent in the experimental process" (21) A.

The Particle Data Group itself warns about the effect in its own publication: "There is a psychological danger in preparing tables of 'right' answers," and "A result can disagree with the average of all previous experiments by five standard deviations and still be right!" (22) A.

Now the quantitative test, and the honest result. Klein and Roodman note first that PDG history plots are "by construction correlated" and "do not by themselves necessarily indicate that there is any bias" (21) A. They therefore tested each measurement against the published average that existed when it was made. Their finding: "the measurements do tend to cluster nearer the prior published averages than the final value," with chi-squared of 131.2 for 83 degrees of freedom against prior averages versus 249.7 for about 82 degrees of freedom against the final average — but they add that "the effect is not striking" (21) A. They note explicitly that clustering would not prove bias, since unrecognised systematic errors could produce it (21).

So: the effect is measured, it is in the direction the critique predicts, and the authors who measured it decline to call it strong. Any use of this material that drops the second half is misrepresenting the source.

Blind analysis is the field's institutional response, and it is real A. Klein and Roodman define it as "a method that hides some aspect of the data or result to prevent experimenter's bias" (21), date its origin in particle physics to about 1990 with BNL E791, and list adoption across E888, NOMAD, KTeV, E871, BABAR, SNO, Soudan 2, E787, BNL g-2, LIGO and MiniBooNE (21). Reserved-data fractions are documented: 10 percent for E888, 20 percent for NOMAD's first run, 30 percent for SNO, 10 percent for LIGO, 0.5 percent for MiniBooNE (21). The Fairbank niobium case shows the magnitude a blind procedure can catch: pre-unblinding charges of (-0.343 +/- 0.011)e, (0.001 +/- 0.033)e and (+0.328 +/- 0.007)e became (+0.189 +/- 0.02)e and (+0.253 +/- 0.02)e after the random offset was removed (22) A.

The chronology matters for attribution: blind analysis predates the Klein-Roodman clustering analysis by about fifteen years, so it was not adopted in response to that finding. See Tier D.

Named Critics and the Field's Rebuttals#

Hossenfelder. Her strongest claim, in a peer-reviewable venue, is that naturalness arguments invoke probability without a distribution and are therefore "either ill-defined or meaningless" (4) B. Her concrete empirical claim is that "the data delivered by the LHC has now ruled out a technically natural explanation for the Higgs mass" (4). Her collider-specific cost-benefit argument is not in that paper; see Tier D.

Woit. On string theory: it "not only makes no predictions about physical phenomena at experimentally accessible energies, it makes no predictions whatsoever," and "cannot be falsified by any conceivable experimental result" (39) B. His sociological claim is that junior researchers are pressed "in a brutal job market to work on the latest fad in string theory," with the recommendation that "Funding agencies should stop supporting theorists who propose to continue working on the same ideas as everyone" (39) B. His remedy is not retrenchment but diversification: "Particle theorists should be exploring a wide range of alternatives to string theory" (39).

The field's rebuttals, from their own words.

Giudice holds that "Naturalness is a well-defined concept rooted in the logic of effective quantum field theory" and "an inescapable consequence of the ingredients generally used to construct effective field theories" (2) B. His core counter to the retirement argument is the diagnosis analogy quoted above (2). His justification for continuing is explicitly not naturalness: "One sure priority is the research program in Higgs physics"; "Missing the opportunity to study in depth the mechanism of electroweak breaking would be like giving up the exploration of a new continent"; "exploration is the only possible answer to our thirst for knowledge" (2) A. He also concedes the epistemic position candidly: "Today we lack the historical perspective to see through the fog of the present state of healthy confusion" (2).

Arkani-Hamed, in a CERN EP newsletter interview: "The regions of parameter space in supersymmetric theories that have been ruled out by the LHC are no cause for worry"; "I am no more concerned about low energy supersymmetry now than I was before the operation of the LHC began"; and "there is no nightmare scenario in the case of the LHC: both answers lead to interesting paths" (41) B. He also states the conditional honestly: "If no superpartners are discovered, we will have to find an alternative solution to the hierarchy problem" (41).

David Gross, on whether non-empirical criteria can confirm a theory, drew a line that critics of the field do not always credit him with. Asked about Richard Dawid's criteria for non-empirical confirmation, Gross offered: "Let's give an operational definition of confidence: I will continue to work on it," adding "Not for science" (40) A. The field's most prominent defender of string theory declined to call non-empirical confirmation scientific confirmation. Dawid's own framing — "non-empirical confirmation is part of science, and has been part of science for quite some time" — is opposed by Rovelli from inside physics: it "takes away one of the tools — maybe the soul itself — of scientific thinking," and "String theory is not, say, 10 percent as confirmed as atomic theory; the two have different statuses entirely" (40) B.

Craig and Feng are quoted above (5, 8) and constitute the substantive naturalness rebuttal: the problem is distinct from any particular solution, and the thresholds were never derivable, so their movement is not evidence of bad faith (5, 8) B.

Structural Inferences#

Three, each with the Tier A facts it rests on and what would falsify it. None is a finding.

C-T1. A criterion with a pre-registered falsification condition met that condition, and the field's response preserved the criterion's motivating role while conceding its predictive content. C

Rests on: Giudice 2008 stating that if the LHC finds no new TeV-scale phenomena "the naturalness criterion would fail," with Lambda < 1.0 TeV (1); the exclusions (11); Giudice 2017 holding that naturalness "cannot simply be ignored" (2); Craig 2022 conceding SUSY "did not appear where naturalness arguments led us to expect it" while denying "naturalness itself has failed" (5); and the 2019/2020 Briefing Book calling the hierarchy problem "a strong argument" after the first condition was met (36).

Falsified by: a strategy or funding document between 2017 and 2025 that states the naturalness criterion's TeV-scale prediction failed and justifies the machine on other grounds alone. The 2023 P5 report and the 2025 FCC manifesto come close by omission (30, 38) but neither states the failure. Also weakened by: split supersymmetry (9), which shows the retreat was constructed in 2004, before the data, and therefore cannot be characterised as post hoc.

C-T2. The technical vocabulary of a contested argument was dropped from the US funding document while the argument itself was retained in lay paraphrase. C

Rests on: the absence of "naturalness," "hierarchy problem," "fine-tuning," "unnatural" and "guaranteed" across all 379,741 characters of the 2023 P5 report, alongside its statements that "We do not know why the Higgs boson mass should be as low as it is" and that a "fantastical accident is required to accommodate the observed mass of the Higgs boson" (30); contrasted with the 2019 FCC CDR and Briefing Book, which use the terms as motivation (35, 36), and the November 2025 European Briefing Book, which restores them (37).

Falsified by: evidence that earlier P5 reports also avoided the terms, which would make the omission house style rather than a change; by a drafting record showing an editorial accessibility rationale; or by the simple observation that P5 reports are written for a policy audience and systematically avoid technical jargon of all kinds. I did not test any of these, and the last is a strong mundane explanation. This inference is the weakest of the three and should not be carried forward without that test.

C-T3. The fine-tuning tolerance implied by current exclusions is about 27 times the tolerance that originally defined the naturalness bound, and the field does not track this as a single published quantity. C

Rests on: the Delta = 10 threshold of 1988 (6, 7); the Delta = 20 of the late 1990s (7); Feng's relation m(gluino) <= 1.4 TeV x (Nmax/100)^(1/2) (8); and the roughly 2.3 TeV gluino exclusion (11). The arithmetic giving Nmax of approximately 270 is mine.

Falsified by: a published figure or table in the SUSY literature tracking the tolerated fine-tuning threshold against time or against exclusion limits — which would show the field does track it; or a demonstration that Feng's GUT-correlated relation does not extrapolate to 2.3 TeV; or a showing that current limits under the relevant simplified-model assumptions do not constrain the natural region as the relation implies. The second and third are live technical objections that a specialist would raise first, and I have not addressed them.

What Would Falsify This Stream's Critique#

Stated so it can be checked, per the research frame.

  1. If the retirement of naturalness as a TeV-scale prediction were stated plainly in the documents that request money for successor machines. Partly satisfied already: the 2023 P5 report and 2025 FCC manifesto contain no naturalness language (30, 38). Not satisfied by the November 2025 European Briefing Book, which uses it as motivation (37).
  2. If the fine-tuning thresholds had been derived rather than stipulated. They were not (6, 7, 8) — but the field says so itself (8), which removes the charge of concealment.
  3. If the community's response to the 750 GeV excess had been proportionate to the published global significances. It was not (12, 13, 14, 15, 16) — and the significances were published correctly, so the failure is behavioural, not statistical.
  4. If PDG historical clustering were a strong, demonstrated effect. It is not; the authors who measured it say "the effect is not striking" (21).
  5. If the no-lose theorems had failed. The Higgs no-lose claim succeeded (33, 34).

Items 4 and 5 are failures of the critique, and they are recorded as such.

Claims Examined and Not Supported#

Tier D. Each of these was investigated and could not be substantiated at the standard the research frame requires.

1. An authoritative count of 750 GeV theory papers. Not obtained. Primary and semi-primary figures are 200 (14, March 2016, mid-episode), 350 (15, Buckley, retrospective), and "hundreds" (16). A secondary source says "about 500" (17). INSPIRE database queries in this session returned no usable total. The file reports the range and does not pick a number.

2. That "no-lose theorem" rhetoric was empty or misleading. Rejected. The 1998 and 2001 no-lose statements concerned the discovery of a Higgs boson (33, 34), and one was discovered in 2012. The 2001 Snowmass summary also recorded its own counterexample for the NMSSM in the same document (34).

3. That naturalness was merely an aesthetic preference. Rejected. Giudice separates aesthetic structural naturalness, which "cannot be used to validate a theory," from the quantitative criterion (1); Williams argues the historical motivations were "not, for example, solely or even primarily based on aesthetics" and grounds the autonomy-of-scales version in effective field theory (3). The aesthetic charge holds against the statistical and multiverse versions, not against the whole criterion.

4. That funding bodies suppressed alternatives to the WIMP paradigm. Not supported. The DOE Second-Generation programme selected 4 WIMP and 1 axion experiment from 13 proposals of which 10 were WIMP and 3 axion (29). Selection tracked submission. Community submission patterns and detector-technology readiness explain the outcome without appeal to capture.

5. A quantified funding comparison between WIMP searches and MOND or modified gravity. Not obtained. No source was found giving comparable dollar figures across those programmes. No disparity is asserted. A dedicated search of agency budget lines would be needed, and the structural difference — direct detection requires capital facilities, modified gravity is largely theory and archival astronomy — may make a dollar comparison meaningless even if assembled.

6. That the SUSY goalposts moved only after exclusions arrived. Rejected. Arkani-Hamed and Dimopoulos abandoned the naturalness motivation in May 2004 (9), seven years before the LHC produced meaningful exclusions.

7. Confirmation-bias clustering in PDG plots as a strong effect. Only partly supported. Klein and Roodman measured clustering toward prior published averages (chi-squared 131.2/83 versus 249.7/82) but wrote that "the effect is not striking," warned that PDG history plots are "by construction correlated," and noted unrecognised systematics as an alternative explanation (21). The qualitative jump pattern for the speed of light and CP violation is documented (21, 22); a strong general effect is not.

8. That blind analysis was adopted in response to the PDG clustering finding. Rejected on chronology. Blind analysis in particle physics dates to about 1990 with BNL E791 (21), roughly fifteen years before the Klein-Roodman analysis. The documented motivations are general experimenter's bias and specific cases such as Dunnington in 1932 and the Fairbank niobium measurements (21, 22).

9. That the swampland programme makes no predictions. Rejected as stated. Danielsson and Van Riet claim a concrete one — "no measurable tensor modes in the CMB" — and state a clean falsification route, a single explicit de Sitter construction (32). Whether the prediction is sharp enough to discriminate is a fair question; the claim that none is offered is false.

10. That the field has not acknowledged SUSY's failure to appear where predicted. Rejected. Craig states it in a community white paper: "supersymmetry did not appear where naturalness arguments led us to expect it" (5).

11. That the 5-sigma threshold is defended inside the field as principled. Rejected. Lyons, a central figure in HEP statistics, calls a uniform 5 sigma "far too blunt a tool" and argues against applying it uniformly (18). The convention's arbitrariness is an in-field position, not an outside criticism.

12. That the 2026 European Strategy Briefing Book conceals the exclusions. Not tested. I read approximately 40 percent of its 500,000 characters and did not reach its beyond-the-Standard-Model chapter, which is where exclusion limits and fine-tuning discussion would normally sit (37). No claim is made about that document's overall balance.

13. Unzicker's central claim. Alexander Unzicker's "The Higgs Fake: How Particle Physicists Fooled the Nobel Committee" (2013, self-published) argues that the Higgs discovery was not genuine (43). That claim falls outside this archive's declared scope, which excludes any assertion that experimental results are fabricated, and I found no primary-literature support for it. Unzicker is named in the brief as an inside-field critic; on the basis of this book he is better described as an outside critic whose central claim this archive does not adopt. Including it would discredit the defensible material by association.

14. Baggott's classification. Jim Baggott, "Farewell to Reality" (2013), is a science writer and chemist by training, not a working particle physicist (44). His critique is not an inside-field critique and is not cited as one here.

15. John Ellis's rebuttal in his own words. Not obtained. The Quanta article (40) quotes George Ellis, a cosmologist who is a critic of non-empirical confirmation — a different person from John Ellis, the CERN theorist named in the brief. The two must not be conflated and no quotation is attributed to John Ellis in this file.

16. Hossenfelder's collider cost-benefit argument in her own words. Not obtained in a retrievable form. Her naturalness critique is in arXiv (4) and is quoted. Her collider-specific argument appears principally on her blog, which is outside this session's network allowlist and could not be fetched. Her position on colliders is therefore characterised in this file only where (4) supports it.

17. Arkani-Hamed's and Gross's fullest defences. Partly obtained. The Arkani-Hamed material comes from a CERN newsletter interview whose date I could not establish (41), and the Gross material from a magazine article (40) rather than from his own writing. Both are their quoted words, but neither is a primary document authored by them, and the file flags this where they are used.

18. A systematic test of whether earlier P5 reports used naturalness vocabulary. Not performed. This is the specific test that inference C-T2 requires, and its absence is why C-T2 is labelled the weakest of the three.
